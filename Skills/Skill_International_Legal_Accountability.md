## ⚖️ 1. The Rome Statute Article 28 Command Responsibility Framework

Under Article 28(a) of the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court (ICC), a military commander or person effectively acting as a military commander is criminally responsible for crimes committed by forces under their effective command and control:

### The 4 Mandatory Legal Pillars of Command Liability:
1. **Effective Command and Control:** The commander exercised actual, operational control over the subordinate forces that committed the crimes (structural or de facto authority).
2. **Knowledge Criterion (*Mens Rea*):** The commander either **knew**, or owing to the circumstances at the time, **should have known** that the forces were committing or about to commit such crimes.
   - *Evidence Required:* Prior UN warning reports, internal operational debriefs, public media coverage of ongoing atrocities, and repeated patterns of civilian strikes across operational theaters.
3. **Failure to Prevent or Repress:** The commander failed to take all necessary and reasonable measures within their power to prevent or halt the commission of the crimes.
   - *Evidence Required:* Lack of cease-fire directives, continued replenishment of ordnance (e.g. thermobaric ODAB-500PM, cluster bombs, aviation fuel), and repeated deployment of the same strike units (e.g., 33rd LID, 99th LID).
4. **Failure to Submit to Authorities:** The commander failed to submit the matter to competent authorities for independent investigation and prosecution.
